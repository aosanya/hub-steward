Change a workflow or a control

The designer changes how the agency works: a workflow, its steps and its records.

1. Opens the workflows

Workflows lists the agency's workflows as cards, each with its steps, the roles that act in it, the objectives it serves and whether it is in use, and an Actions menu on each. The built-in Cancel is marked as a default. Under the cards sit the orders under way, with the version each is on and a Stop button.

Open the mockup

2. Opens one workflow

Opening Group order shows its steps as cards, in order, each with its kind, its one actor, its action and the form it asks.

Open the mockup

3. Edits a workflow

Edit from the Actions menu opens the editor on the Group order, with three tabs on one draft: Workflow holds the name and what it is for, Steps holds one card for each step in order, and Records holds the lists the workflow keeps beside its steps, such as Payments, added by the Group coordinator and seen by each Farmer for their own row. The Actor list is the agency's own roles and groups. Save returns to the list with a notice that the change waits in the draft.

Open the mockup

4. Adds a workflow

Add a workflow opens the same three tabs empty. A workflow is a name and what it is for; each step has a name, a kind, one actor, an action and a form, added from a card that says Add a step; a record is added with the form each row is filled in with and who adds rows.

Open the mockup

5. Asks for a cancel workflow

The built-in Cancel cancels at once. Edit on it opens an ask that replaces it with a cancel workflow of the agency's own, and the same can be asked in the chat. The filled form lands in the content area on the same tabs: the Farmer fills a form saying why, then the Supplier says yes or no, and the workflow keeps no record. Save records it.

Open the mockup

6. Removes a workflow

Remove in the Actions menu asks the agency. While a test scenario mirrors the workflow, the agency refuses: the workflow stays, the chat says why, and the scenarios that mirror it are listed in the content area.

Open the mockup

7. Sets how long a waiting step may wait

On a step where a person fills a form or says yes or no, the designer sets a time limit and picks the workflow that cancels the order when the limit passes. An order that waits too long closes and its cancel workflow starts.

Open the mockup

Result: A workflow or a control is changed, added or removed in the draft, and nothing reaches members until it is published.
